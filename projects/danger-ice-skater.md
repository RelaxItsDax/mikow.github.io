## Overview

![Gameplay in Danger: Ice Skater](assets/img/danger/danger-gameplay.png)

Danger: Ice Skater was my team's submission to the 2025 GMTK Game Jam. The theme of the jam was "Loop". We made this game over **97 hours**, with **a team of 12 people** working on it.

This is the first game that I made that I'm truly proud of. I'm extremely happy with how it went, even today. I think it was a great example of doing things in a game jam right, and I learned so much from it. 

## Context

This was the first game jam I did after starting work at the Imaging Research Center. That internship **taught me a lot about git workflows, project management, and collaboration between developers**. This jam was the first time I properly applied it.

## What I did

![Gameplay in Danger: Ice Skater](assets/img/danger/danger-gameplay-2.png)

On this project, I was a gameplay programmer. I implemented: 
- the enemy spawning system, 
- the round progression systems, 
- a lot of the UI,
- and enemy AI (sorry). 

I also handled a LOT of code reviews, git fixing, and project cleaning. During the jam itself, I contributed around 10 hours of work every day.
## Results

Over the four days of development, we had **111 pull requests** created and closed, rounding out everything we wanted to do. 

Scoring wise, there were around 10,000 projects submitted, and we ended up in the **top 7% for enjoyment**, and in the **top 10% overall**. It was a huge success, especially for my first real game jam with a good team.

Here is our credits page. Thanks to everyone involved!
![Gameplay in Danger: Ice Skater](assets/img/danger/danger-credits.png)

## Takeaways

This was the first time that I worked on a big team and had things go well. I took away **tons of project management tips**, as well as just general experience for working on games, especially when sprinting or in a short time frame.

While I didn't really learn any new development skills, this project served to reinforce what needed practicing in my skill set. Overall, it was an amazing experience, one that I keep bringing up as a great example of my work!
